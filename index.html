Screening & Risk Stratification
Gather

Risk stratification — Axis 1: Likelihood patient has AGS

Higher pre-test probabilityRecalled tick bite (~80–86%)1 + endemic residence + delayed 2–6 h reactions2
Endemic (high-seroprevalence) statesAR ~31%, MO ~26%3; SE / Mid-Atlantic / Midwest / East-Central
Supportive historyOutdoor exposure, nocturnal GI symptoms, multiple unexplained allergies, prior red-meat/dairy/gelatin reactions2,4
Lower probabilityNo tick exposure, non-endemic, immediate (not delayed) reactions2

Axis 2: Diagnostic confidence (α-gal IgE)

< 0.1 kU/LNegative2
≥ 0.1 kU/LSuggestive / positive2
≥ 2 kU/L or > 2% total IgE>50% meat-challenge positivity5
≥ 5.5 kU/L or ratio ≥ 2.12%≥95% positive predictive value6

Axis 3: Perioperative severity risk (drives management)

HIGHIV/parenteral animal-derived agents (gelatin hemostatics — Floseal/Surgiflo/Gelfoam, bovine/porcine thrombin, porcine heparin) + any cofactor (exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs) or prior anaphylaxis2,7,8
MODERATETopical/implanted animal-derived material (collagen scaffolds, gut sutures) or oral gelatin excipients; positive IgE without high-risk route7,8
LOWEROnly non-mammalian agents planned (HA fillers, synthetic sutures/hemostatics, HPMC capsules); sensitized but avoidable exposure7

Stratify, do not exclude

Titer and prior reaction severity do not predict future severity. Cofactors lower the threshold, and reactions are inconsistent between exposures. Use the axes to set observation length and premedication — not to deny care.2

Obtain from every suspected patient

  • Known triggers (mammalian meat, dairy, gelatin, meds)
  • Prior reactions — severity and exact timing
  • Previously tolerated products / materials
  • Cofactors: exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs

Preparedness & Monitoring
Prepare

Tier-based observation & premedication (unavoidable mammalian agent)

Expert-opinion / extrapolated — no validated tier-specific protocol exists. Durations derive from the 2–6 h (up to ~10 h) delayed-reaction window, not fixed cutoffs.2,9

HIGH

Premedication: corticosteroid + H1 (± H2) antihistamine before the agent; A/I supervision; IM epinephrine drawn up.2,10

Observation: continuous monitoring spanning the full delayed window (≥6 h, extend toward ~10 h); low threshold for overnight admission — especially after severe initial reaction or repeat epinephrine.9,11

Prefer route/product substitution (SC LMWH over IV UFH) over reliance on premedication.2

MODERATE

Premedication: consider corticosteroid + antihistamine per A/I; epinephrine available.2

Observation: prolonged monitoring through the delayed window; do not discharge at the standard 1 h biphasic mark.9,11

LOWER

Premedication: not routinely indicated.7

Observation: routine postoperative monitoring; counsel on delayed symptoms and epinephrine use.9

Adjuncts (antihistamines, corticosteroids) do not treat or prevent IgE-mediated anaphylaxis — IM epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg (max 0.3 mg peds / 0.5 mg adult) remains first-line if a reaction occurs.11

Observation principle

Observe 2–6 h (longer if a reaction is treated).

Ready at discharge

  • Epinephrine autoinjector when appropriate
  • Written delayed-reaction precautions
